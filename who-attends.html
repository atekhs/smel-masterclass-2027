The Masterclass's significance lies in the position of its delegates. Principals set strategic direction, but that direction becomes real only through the officials who prepare memos, brief their principals, organise state visits, manage budgets, secure venues and oversee staff. A strong layer of such officials keeps institutions steady through political change. When that layer is underdeveloped, even the best intentions falter.

What delegates gain

  • Strategic elevation: a shift from managing tasks to shaping institutional direction.
  • Confidence at the top table: the frameworks, vocabulary and presence to advise principals and engage counterparts as equals.
  • Crisis readiness: practised responses to the security, reputational and operational shocks that define careers.
  • Protocol and diplomatic polish: precision in the ceremonies and engagements through which institutions are judged.
  • A continental network: peers across Africa facing the same challenges, available long after the programme ends.
  • Recognised credentials: a Certificate of Completion in Strategic Management and Executive Leadership, and eligibility for the Ubumwe Laurels.

For sponsoring institutions, the return outlasts the six days: stronger strategy documents, sharper policy advice, more disciplined protocol, better-managed crises, and officials motivated by the recognition that their institution invested in them.

Primary target participants

CategoryTypical titlesWhy they need the Masterclass
Senior public servants of MDAsPermanent Secretaries, Directors-General, Directors, Deputy Directors, Heads of Department, Supervisors of core unitsThey hold institutional memory and run the machinery of government; they convert policy into programmes and budgets.
Political appointeesSenior Special Assistants and Special Advisers, Senior Legislative Aides, Chiefs of Staff, Executive Secretaries of agencies and commissions, Heads of Protocol UnitsThey advise principals, manage access and agendas, and must deliver results within a limited political term.
Legislative administrationClerks and Deputy Clerks, Directors of legislative committees, Heads of legislative research and budget officesThey shape laws, oversight and appropriation, and the quality of evidence legislators rely on.
Protocol and diplomatic officersChiefs and Directors of Protocol, State House and Government House protocol officers, Foreign Service officers, consular and liaison officersThey manage the ceremonies, visits and engagements by which governments and companies are judged.
Security personnel and consultantsSenior officers of the armed forces, police, intelligence, civil defence, immigration and customs; security advisers to principals; private security and risk consultantsSecurity is now strategic; these leaders need management, crisis-leadership and stakeholder skills alongside operational expertise.
Corporate administrative leadershipCompany Secretaries, Chief Administrative Officers, Administrative and Office Managers, Heads of Corporate Affairs, Government Relations and Executive OfficesThey run the operational core of companies and manage relations with government, regulators and the public.
C-Suite executivesManaging Directors, CEOs, COOs, CFOs, Chief Strategy Officers of public and private enterprisesThey set organisational strategy and benefit from peer exchange with public-sector counterparts.
